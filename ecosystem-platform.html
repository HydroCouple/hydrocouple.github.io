Artificial intelligence

AI and physics, coupled

AI multiplies physics-based modeling. The ecosystem is built so the two reinforce each other: agents operate the models, learning algorithms control the infrastructure, and the physics engines supply the trustworthy, mass-conserving simulation that AI needs to train against and be checked by.

Agents that build and run models

Through openswmm.mcp, AI agents assemble networks, edit parameters, run simulations, and interrogate results over the Model Context Protocol: expert modeling workflows expressed in plain language.

Execution platform

Simulations on compute you control

The HydroCouple execution platform runs SWMM simulations and HydroCouple compositions durably on your own infrastructure, for people, the desktop application and AI agents. A control plane decides what runs; an agent in your account or site does the running.

Your compute, your data

The control plane records requests, enforces permissions, budgets and approvals, and leases work. An execution agent inside your account or site opens every connection itself, runs work on your scheduler (a local pool or Kubernetes) and writes results to your own storage: S3, Azure Blob Storage, Google Cloud Storage or files.

Runs you can trace and repeat

Runners execute approved, versioned workflows next to the engine. They verify every input, then write a HydroCouple run manifest and an attempt manifest with provenance. Fenced leases let the platform recover from a lost agent without publishing a result twice, and the manifests load into the water-ontology data model.

Studies at scale, for people and agents

Ask-and-tell studies lease candidates in chunks to evaluator pools running openswmm.gymnasium optimizers, keeping lineage and the Pareto set. The same client serves the desktop application, the command line and AI agents working through openswmm.mcp.

Built today: the contracts, the durable core, packaging and runners on the real engine, studies with evaluator pools, and Kubernetes installs with modules for AWS, Azure and Google Cloud. Next: the platform toolset for the MCP server.
